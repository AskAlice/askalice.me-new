1. Purpose and scope

The hardware draws images in the air with two rows of HD107S LEDs. The firmware times each column from rotation angle rather than wall-clock time, so images don't squish or stretch as the spin speed changes. One controller design serves two products:

Product LED board Rotation centre
Poi (pair) led-bar-poi: one arm, 165 LEDs per face, flex Your hand (tether)
Bike wheel led-bar-wheel-<size>: two arms, LEDs on both faces of each arm Axle, at the middle of the bar

In scope: schematics, PCBs, fab outputs, mechanical concept, BLE upload protocol, Android uploader. Out of scope: controller firmware. docs/PROTOCOL.md is the contract it must implement.

Battery current at the cap is about 3.2 A (3.8 A at 3.0 V), well inside every listed cell's rating. The static current matters: with the rail on and nothing lit the LEDs alone take 1.65 W, so the firmware must drop LED_EN whenever the poi stops spinning, not just send black.

3. LED boards (rev C: 3.0 mm pitch, LEDs on both faces)

3.1 Construction

Every LED board carries HD107S-2020 LEDs on both faces, reflowed at the fab (double-sided SMT):

  HD107S-2020 (face A) / silk / F.Cu ─ In1 GND ─ In2 VLED ─ B.Cu / silk / HD107S-2020 (face B)
Board Substrate Why
led-bar-poi 4-layer polyimide flex, 0.3 mm Flexible, as you asked; the acrylic tube is the structure, so the strip only has to hang in it (§6). FR4 0.2 mm stiffener under the connector only.
led-bar-wheel-* 1.6 mm 4-layer FR4 The bar spans the wheel with nothing around it, so it must be its own spine.
led-bar-poi-seg (2 per poi) + poi-root-adapter 4-layer polyimide flex, 0.3 mm; adapter 0.8 mm 4-layer FR4 Same poi strip cut into two identical 251 mm segments so JLCPCB can assemble it (see below). The rigid adapter carries the GH-10, anchors the strip in the sled and is its own stiffener.

Density. Pitch is 3.0 mm (≈ 333 LEDs/m), the tightest this layout passes DRC at. Each LED keeps its own 100 nF capacitor, two data-hop vias and two plane vias, and face B sits half a pitch along so the two faces' vias never collide. Below 3.0 mm the vias of the two faces start to touch; going denser would need via-in-pad or a 6-layer stack.

Segmented poi flex (JLCPCB build). led-bar-poi stays as the one-piece option (517.5 mm) for assemblers that take 520 mm flex. For JLCPCB (assembly up to 460 × 500 mm, regular flex up to 490 mm) the poi uses poi-root-adapter + 2 × led-bar-poi-seg:

  • Segment: 251.0 × 20 mm, same stack, LED/cap/via scheme as led-bar-poi, 83 LEDs per face (166 per face per poi, about 498 mm of LEDs). Face A LED k at x = 1.75 + 3k, face B at x = 3.25 + 3k (x from the segment's root edge).
  • Lap joint: segment 2 lies on top of segment 1, both face A up. Its root edge sits at x = 249.0 of segment 1 (= 83 × 3.0 mm), so the overlap is 2.0 mm (249.0 to 251.0) and both faces continue at exactly 3.0 mm pitch. In the overlap only the touching faces are LED-free: segment 1's tip has joint pads on F.Cu, segment 2's root has the same pads on B.Cu. The outward faces keep their LEDs: segment 2's face-A LED 0 sits inside the overlap on top, segment 1's last face-B LED sits inside it underneath.
  • Why 2.0 mm, not 2.5–3 mm: the outward-face LEDs next to the overlap (segment 1's last face-A LED, segment 2's face-B LED 0) are 2.0 mm bodies that must clear the mating segment's edge. With the 1.5 mm face stagger the overlap can be at most 2.5 mm minus twice the edge gap; a 0.25 mm gap gives 2.0 mm. A longer overlap would sit an LED body on the other segment's edge. poi_seg.SegGeom.check() enforces this.
  • Identical segments: the tip end is the root end turned 180° (x → L − x, y → −y, F.Cu ↔ B.Cu, face A ↔ face B, inputs ↔ outputs). The generator designs the root copper once and mirrors it, and checks that tip pads = root pads + 249.0 mm.
  • Root input: CLK_A, DAT_A, CLK_B, DAT_B, VLED, GND on segment 1's root pads. The faces need separate clocks after LED 0 because each LED re-drives the clock on CO. poi-root-adapter (22 × 20 mm) ties the single CLK from the GH-10 (pinout §4 unchanged) to both CLK_A and CLK_B, carries 2 × 22 µF + 100 nF, and is the mechanical anchor: board edge plus two Ø 2.2 mm holes into the sled. Its tip has the same F.Cu pads as a segment tip.
  • Last segment's tip pads: leave them unsoldered and cover them with Kapton tape or coverlay. The tip-cap pins go through its two Ø 1.0 mm alignment holes.

Each power net gets two pads (4.4 mm of VLED and 5.0 mm of GND across the strip), each with two same-net plane vias, so 2 A is not a problem. Signal pads have no vias. Signal vias sit in the y = ±1.6 mm gaps and are tented on both faces, under the mating segment's solder mask.

Joint assembly (per poi, one joint plus the adapter joint, which is made the same way):

  1. JLCPCB delivers segments fully assembled; the joint pads are bare ENIG (no paste) on purpose.
  2. Jig: an aluminium plate with two Ø 1.0 mm pins 13.2 mm apart, plus a 3 mm wide × 1.5 mm deep relief channel along the LED row (face-B LEDs underneath). Put segment 1 face A up over the pins through its tip holes. Apply a thin layer of SnBi58 low-temperature paste (or 0.1 mm preform) to its tip pads. Put segment 2 face A up over the same pins through its root holes. The silk ticks show where each edge should land.
  3. Reflow at about 170–180 °C: hot-bar with a relieved thermode (it must not press on segment 2's face-A LED 0 in the overlap) for 5–8 s, or heat the jig plate on a hot plate. SnBi melts at 138 °C, so the LEDs' SAC305 joints (217 °C) do not reflow a third time.
  4. Test before closing: DMM check that VLED–GND is not shorted and that each signal pad pair has continuity. Then power from the adapter, drive 166 LEDs on each face (DAT_A and DAT_B), and confirm both chains light past LED 82 → 83.
  5. Wick low-viscosity flexible underfill or edge-bond epoxy along both cut edges, keeping it off the LED lenses. Then wrap the overlap with 25 µm polyimide tape or a coverlay strip, leaving the overlap LEDs uncovered. Do the same for the adapter joint, and cover the last segment's unused tip pads.

3.2 Electrical

  • Feed point: each LED string starts at the end of its board where the connector is (poi: the root end by the hand; wheel: the hub end of each arm, right beside the controller) and runs outward.
  • Parallel data: one shared CLK, with DAT_A on face A and DAT_B on face B. The controller clocks both in the same SPI transfer.
  • Data hops: the 2020 package runs clock and data in opposite directions. Each data link drops through a via and hops one pitch on an inner layer, alternating layers because neighbouring hops overlap.
  • Stagger: face B is offset +pitch/2 (1.5 mm). Firmware samples face B's image at r + 1.5 mm.
  • Decoupling: 100 nF per LED and 10 µF every 12 LEDs; VLED and GND reach every LED through the planes.
  • Refresh: a 165-LED lane is 675 bytes. At 30 MHz that is 180 µs per column, well inside the 926 µs a column lasts at 3 rev/s with 360 columns.

3.4 Wheel hub

The wheel needs no cable string: the controller and a LiPo sit at the hub, on the bar itself.

  • Hub plate: 40 mm wide with a Ø24 mm hole that slides over the hub's axle end while the wheel is out of the frame; the bar sits between the hub flange and the locknut, so dropout spacing doesn't change.
  • Wings: two 26 × 80 mm wings run perpendicular to the bar. The +y wing carries the controller (printed tray on four M3 holes), the −y wing carries the LiPo. Mass-balance them: weigh controller + tray and battery + tray and add a small counterweight to the lighter side, or the wheel will shake at speed.
  • Battery: a 1S LiPo pouch (e.g. 503450, ~1000 mAh, ~20 g) on the controller's JST PH battery input.
  • Wiring: two 50 mm GH-10 jumpers from controller ports 1 and 2 to the arm connectors beside the hole.
  • Side: on a front wheel, the side without the disc rotor. On a rear wheel, rim-brake wheels only, non-drive side; a rear disc wheel has no free side. Check fork clearance: wings plus trays stand about 10 mm proud of the bar.
  • Spoke clips: four Ø3.2 mm holes along the arms take printed spoke clips.

3.5 Rotation sensing (variable speed)

Speed is never assumed. The IMU gyro on the controller measures spin rate continuously (3.33 kHz FIFO), and firmware fires each column when the integrated angle crosses the next column boundary, so the image keeps its shape whether the poi slows down or the bike speeds up. Gyro drift is cancelled with the once-per-revolution gravity signal from the accelerometer.

Case Spin rate Gyro margin (±4000 dps)
Poi, fast spin 3–4 rev/s = 1080–1440 dps 2.8×
700c wheel at 50 km/h 6.6 rev/s = 2380 dps 1.7× (limit ≈ 84 km/h)
16" wheel at 25 km/h 5.7 rev/s = 2060 dps 1.9× (limit ≈ 48 km/h)

The gyro reads the rotation of the whole rigid body, so it works wherever the controller is mounted (poi handle end or wheel hub).

IMU choice and mounting.

Option Accelerometer Mount Accelerometer usable to (IMU 0.3 m from the hand)
LSM6DSV16X (default) ±16 g only rotated 45° in the board plane (imu_mount_deg = 45) 22.6 g radial ≈ 4.3 rev/s (≈ 4.1 rev/s with gravity)
LSM6DSV320X (premium) ±16 g + ±320 g channel 0° or 45° any spin (high-g channel takes over per sample)

The firmware receives samples in the sensor frame and rotates x/y into body axes by imu_mount_deg. Saturation is judged per sensor axis before the rotation (|a| ≥ 15.5 g), because after a 45° rotation both body axes, including the tangential one that carries the gravity phase, mix both sensor axes. With the LSM6DSV16X a saturated sample is left out of the accelerometer fits (the gyro still integrates it):

  • the gravity-phase correction uses the unsaturated part of each revolution (if at least half of it is clean);
  • the centre / circularity estimate skips any revolution with saturated samples, and the display mode decided during spin-up is held (mode_hold, with a saturated sample count) until clean revolutions return;
  • with the whole revolution saturated (above ≈ 4.3 rev/s on a 0.3 m tether) the angle runs on the gyro alone, with the gyro-bias term learned at lower speed. Simulated 2 → 5 rev/s ramp: angle error < 0.3°.

3.6 Orbit detection: disc, annulus or banner

The same IMU also measures where the rotation centre is and how circular the path is, so the firmware picks how to draw (implemented in firmware/core, tested on synthetic motion):

  • Centre distance: over one revolution the radial specific force averages to −ω²·d (gravity cancels), so the gyro rate ω and the mean radial acceleration give the signed distance d from the IMU to the centre. With the default LSM6DSV16X @45° this works up to ≈ 22.6 g radial; faster revolutions are skipped and the last decision is held (§3.5). The LSM6DSV320X's ±320 g channel keeps it working at any speed.
  • Circularity: a hand that moves in its own circle (extensions, "flowers") adds acceleration at other harmonics of the spin and changes d from turn to turn. Both are turned into a hand-radius estimate and a 0–1 circularity score.
  • Modes (with hysteresis, two revolutions to switch):
Mode When What is drawn
DISC circular and the centre within 40 mm of LED 0 (poi held at the strip root), or for a wheel within 40 mm of the hub Full circle: a raster image fills a disc whose diameter is twice the strip's length
ANNULUS circular, centre further away (poi on a tether) Polar image with a hole the size of the tether
BANNER path not circular (a torus wider than the display) Columns wrap once per turn; no geometry assumed
IDLE spin below 180 °/s LEDs off, rail cut

Raster images (POVI mode 2, docs/PROTOCOL.md §2.1) are ordinary square bitmaps, so the same file works in DISC and ANNULUS mode. Limitation: a hand moving slowly (≈1 Hz) is nearly invisible to the accelerometer.

5.2 Power-off behaviour

There is no mechanical switch. - Off state: firmware holds LED_EN low (boost off, true disconnect) and puts the ESP32 into deep sleep. Drain is about 80–120 µA without a card (LDO Iq 55 µA, divider 4 µA, IMU in power-down, ESP deep sleep). A microSD card on the always-on 3.3 V rail adds 50–250 µA of standby current. With a card fitted, expect roughly 1–2 years of shelf life on a 2800 mAh cell. Rev C should switch the card's supply. - Storage: for long-term storage, unplug the JST PH battery connector.

6. Poi chassis: plain clear tube with bonded bottle-neck ends

The tube itself is not threaded. Each end gets a short PET bottle-preform neck (38 mm 2-start finish) bonded over the tube, so the caps screw onto a moulded thread instead of a thread cut into acrylic. This removes the turning and threading step, keeps the tube wall full thickness (acrylic is notch-sensitive), and gives an injection-moulded, liner-sealed thread for pennies. Sources: docs/research/2026-10-sourcing.md §4 (tubes) and docs/research/2026-10-comparables-preforms.md §2 (necks, adhesives).

Small clear tubes are extruded, not cast, and hold about ±0.3–0.5 mm on the OD. Extruded acrylic is prone to stress cracking, so polycarbonate in the same size is the safer choice for a spinning prop; it is just as clear but scratches more easily.

6.3 What's inside and how it is held

  • Order from the hand: string-holder cap → controller sled → cell → LED flex → tip cap.
  • Sled: a printed carrier holding the controller and the tabbed cell. Two keys on the sled engage the string-holder end so the USB-C window and button line up with the board.
  • Flex: the root (or the root adapter of the segmented strip, §3.1) clips into the sled, the tip clips onto a pin in the tip cap. Centrifugal force pulls the flex taut when spinning; at rest, two printed TPU edge rails keep it centred, with about 9 mm of air between the LEDs and the wall on each face.
  • Load path: everything pushes outward onto the tip cap: about 44 N at 3 rev/s, 78 N at 4 rev/s. The tube carries it back through the two bonded necks to the string-holder cap (tube wall cross-section about 285 mm², so stress in the tube is well under 1 MPa).

6.4 Caps

String-holder cap (38 mm 2-start internal thread, printed or turned): - Liner or O-ring seal on the neck's lip (IP67 with the cap on). - M5 swivel eye for the leash (or your string holder of choice). - USB-C behind the cap: unscrew to charge, so no port is open to the weather. A flexure button over SW1 (BOOT, doubles as power) and an IP67 ePTFE acoustic vent for the microphone. - Material: SLS PA12 or turned aluminium. Design load 400 N; proof-test with a 40 kg hanging load.

Tip cap: an off-the-shelf 38 mm cap with a pin for the flex tip, or a printed one, plus a TPU bumper ring (Ø 38 mm) against floor strikes.

6.5 Wheel

No tube; the rigid bar is the structure. Conformal-coat both faces and clip a clear PETG cover over each arm. Controller and LiPo ride on the hub wings (§3.4).

E-bike wheels: power from the bike's battery. The LEDs turn with the wheel and the battery doesn't, so the power has to cross a rotating joint. Options, best first:

Option How For Against
Keep the hub LiPo, charge it from the bike A small 1S LiPo on the wing (§3.4); a 36/48 V → 5 V buck on the frame feeds a USB-C lead you plug in when parked No moving contacts, works on every hub Still a battery on the wheel
Inductive (wireless) coupler Stationary coil on the frame side of the hub (fed by a 36/48 V → 12 V buck), rotating coil on the wheel side, rectifier + 5 V boost on the controller Sealed, no wear, no wires to tangle, no battery on the wheel 10–15 W through a small air gap needs a custom coil pair; about 70–80 % efficient
Slip ring A through-bore slip ring around the axle, buck converter on the frame Simple electrically Hub motors leave no room for one (the motor cables already run out of the axle); brush wear, water

Don't run a wire from the frame to the wheel without one of these: it wraps around the axle on the first revolution. Whatever the option, step the bike's 36/48 V down on the frame side with a fused, isolated buck converter, and keep the LED current cap (2 A at 5 V per wheel side).

6.6 Version 1: off-the-shelf strip, no custom LED board

A first version can skip the custom 3 mm-pitch flex entirely:

  • LEDs: one 1 m, 144/m clocked strip (APA102 or HD107S, 5050) folded in half, the two halves back to back = 72 LEDs per face at 6.9 mm pitch (the original prototype layout). There is no off-the-shelf 300/m+ clocked strip and no double-sided strip (docs/research), so 144/m 5050 is the practical choice.
  • Denser v1 option: SuperLightingLED's APA102-2020 strip is 250/m (≈4.0 mm pitch), 3 mm wide, sold in 0.5 m semi-rigid pieces. Two pieces glued back to back on the spine give 125 LEDs per face at 4.0 mm pitch with no fold (POVI pitch_um = 4000). The price is not listed, so get a quote before choosing it over the 144/m strip.
  • Spine: a printed or aluminium flat bar glued between the two halves (structural acrylic adhesive or VHB), which also stops the strip whipping and cracking its joints. The fold joint at the tip is protected by the tip cap.
  • IP67: the strip and spine go inside the same tube with bonded bottle-neck ends; the caps seal on the neck; the USB-C port sits under the string-holder cap (unscrew to charge) so no open port faces the outside; the microphone breathes through an IP67 ePTFE acoustic vent in that cap. Test with a 30 min, 1 m immersion before sale.
  • Electronics: the same controller and firmware. The firmware geometry changes to 72 LEDs at 6.94 mm pitch (POVI pitch_um = 6944); raster images adapt automatically.
  • Cost: see docs/calc/unit_cost.py (variant v1): about $223 per pair at 100 pairs and $179 at 1,000, against $262 and $196 for the custom flex. At a $499 retail price that is about 47 % gross margin at 100 pairs and 56 % at 1,000. Estimates; the main levers are the controller BOM, the tube and caps, assembly time and the tariff on imported parts.

7. Firmware contract (summary)

  • Angle tracking: integrate gyro Z from the IMU FIFO (LSM6DSV16X @45°, or LSM6DSV320X) at 3.33 kHz to get angle θ. Correct drift with the gravity phase from the accelerometer: band-pass at the spin frequency, then a PLL.
  • Column timing: fire column c when θ crosses c · 360°/C. Spin direction picks the playback order.
  • Faces: face B mirrors the column order (it is seen from behind) and uses radius r_k + pitch/2. On wheels, arm 2 plays at c + C/2.
  • Brightness: keep the HD107S global brightness at 31 and scale RGB in firmware, which avoids the global-brightness PWM flicker. The 5 V current cap is enforced per column.
  • Images and upload: POVI files live at /pov/*.povi on the SD card. The BLE upload protocol and file format are defined in docs/PROTOCOL.md.
  • Shared core: everything that decides what the LEDs show is the portable C library firmware/core (no malloc, no libm): rotation tracking, orbit detection (§3.6), image sampling, show effects, microphone onset detection and the ESP-NOW codec. The ESP-IDF app only adds drivers. The same code compiles to WASM for the simulator (sim/), so the simulator shows exactly what the device would.
  • Show modes: IMAGE (one image), PLAYLIST (timed), MUSIC. In MUSIC mode a rule table maps events to effects: by default GM drums on channel 10 (kick → next image, snare → white flash, hats → brightness pulse), melody notes → hue shift, microphone beats → next image. Rules can be changed per show.
  • Music over ESP-NOW (docs/ESPNOW.md): a conductor (an ESP32-S3 dev board with an SD card and an I2S amplifier or line out) plays song.wav and broadcasts the matching song.mid as MIDI packets with a shared clock; every poi schedules effects 20 ms ahead so all of them fire together. A poi can ask the conductor to start a song (TRIGGER_SONG, e.g. a long press on the button). Without a conductor, the on-board microphone drives the same rules from detected beats.

9.1 Controller routing

The controller is generated in stages: 1. Placement: the PLACE table in controller.py. 2. Power: pours, fan-out vias for every GND / VLED / +3V3 / VBAT pad, and the boost switch node. 3. LED bus: hand-routed from the ESP32 to the level shifter. 4. Everything else: freerouting 1.9.0.

Any connection the autorouter leaves is listed by DRC as unconnected_items. U4's VIN and EN fail on every run; rotating the TPS61022 so those pins face inward is the likely fix. Finish the rest in KiCad's interactive router before ordering, then re-run DRC. The current list is in §9 of the shared spec.

10. Rollback

Every board is reproducible from hardware/generator. Earlier revisions are in git history. Rev A (flex) was withdrawn because folding put the lanes side by side instead of back to back.

KiCad 10 migration (generator 1.1.0): the boards and schematics are now saved in KiCad 10 format and are not readable by KiCad 7/8. To go back, check out the pre-1.1.0 generator, run it on KiCad 7 (/usr/bin/kicad-cli is still installed on the build machine), and remove the /usr/local/bin/kicad-cli and /usr/local/bin/k10 wrappers (tools/setup-kicad10.sh).
